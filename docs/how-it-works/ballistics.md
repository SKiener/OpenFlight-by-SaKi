# Ballistics & Carry

Carry is not measured — it is simulated. OpenFlight integrates the ball's
trajectory from the launch conditions it measured, accounting for drag and the
Magnus force from spin.

Implemented in `src/openflight/ballistics.py`.

## The model

Three forces act on the ball: gravity, aerodynamic drag opposing motion, and
the Magnus lift generated by backspin.

$$
\vec{F} = m\vec{g}
- \tfrac{1}{2}\rho A C_d \lvert\vec{v}\rvert \vec{v}
+ \tfrac{1}{2}\rho A C_l \lvert\vec{v}\rvert^2 \hat{n}
$$

The coefficients depend on the **spin parameter** $S_p = r\omega / v$ — the
ratio of surface speed to translational speed:

$$
C_d = a + b\,S_p + c\,S_p^2
\qquad
C_l = \max\!\left(0,\; d + e\,S_p + f\,S_p^2\right)
$$

Both are second-order polynomials in $S_p$, the form **Ferguson, McNally &
McPhee (2022)** fitted to 1040 measured shots; the coefficients are their
published values. Lift rises with spin, peaks near $S_p \approx 0.52$ and then
falls, which is what the measured data shows and what a saturating form cannot
represent. Above $S_p = 0.75$, the top of the fitted range, both curves are held
at their end value rather than extrapolated.

These parametric forms are consistent with **Bearman & Harvey (1976)** and
**Kensrud & Smith (2018)** for dimpled balls past the drag crisis
(Re ≈ 5×10⁴–2×10⁵), which covers the full range of realistic golf shots.

Spin decays exponentially over the flight:

$$
\omega(t) = \omega_0 \, e^{-\lambda t}
$$

at roughly 4 %/s per **Kiratidis & Leinweber (2018)** — small per second, but it
matters across a six-second flight.

## Integration

Fourth-order Runge-Kutta at 500 Hz. RK4 error is $O(\Delta t^5)$, so at this
step size the integration is effectively exact for the timescales involved;
noticeably larger steps begin to shorten long drives.

The solver returns points every 50 ms rather than every step — integration still
runs at the full rate, but the sampled list keeps WebSocket payloads and session
logs a reasonable size.

Real shots terminate in 5–9 seconds. The 15-second cap exists so a solver
instability or pathological input fails loudly instead of spinning.

## Range-ball drag (opt-in, unvalidated)

`--range-ball-drag-slope S` (default 0.0 = off, allowed 0.0–1.0) raises the drag
coefficient of every ballistic flight above a Reynolds number of
$1.2 \times 10^5$:

$$
\mathrm{Re} = \frac{\rho\, v\, 2r}{\mu}, \qquad
C_d \leftarrow C_d \left(1 + S \cdot \frac{\max(0,\ \mathrm{Re} - 1.2\times10^5)}{10^5}\right)
$$

with $\mu = 1.81\times10^{-5}$ Pa·s. Fast drives brake harder; wedges stay
below the knee and keep today's drag. It is candidate (c1) of the 2026-11-03
flight-model study against this user's Toptracer range data
(`analysis/flugmodell-20261103/aero`, `bericht.md` §6–7): with TT's own launch
values and spin the model flies the driver about 12 m too far, and (c1) at its
fit $S = 0.3546$ removes most of the wood excess (typical drive −10.7 m carry,
−1.4 m apex) without moving the irons of the one session with measured weather.
It **failed** the study's pre-registered rule (peak height and hang time got
worse), so it is a switch for testing, not the model: the default flies exactly
as before. The slope applies to the live carry, the fixed-density export carry
and the carry span alike, and is logged in the session-start config
(`range_ball_drag_slope`, null with `--no-ballistics`).

## Carry variants (session log only)

Every live ballistic carry is flown four more times for the next range
validation against Toptracer, after the shot was published (on the carry-span
worker, so neither the shot nor its span waits): the shot's own launch (ball
speed, both launch angles, spin axis) at the carry's own air density, with

| Variant | Spin | Range-ball drag slope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `tour` | tour table | 0 |
| `range_ball_spin` | range-ball table | 0 |
| `range_ball_spin_drag` | range-ball table | 0.3546 (the study's fit) |
| `range_ball_spin_drag_half` | range-ball table | 0.225 (largest slope that met the study's rule on all data, not cross-validated) |

The tables are used as listed, without `--typical-spin-scale`, and whatever
`--typical-spin-table` and `--range-ball-drag-slope` the live carry runs; a
trusted measured spin stays measured in all four. They integrate at
`CARRY_VARIANT_DT_SECONDS` = 0.01 s, which the study validated against the
live 0.002 s at ≤ 0.011 m carry, so `tour` matches a default live carry to
about 0.01 m. Carry and apex go to the session log's `carry_variants` entry in
metres ([session-log.md](../reference/session-log.md)); nothing reaches the
shot, the screens or the simulators, and a failure logs `null` with a reason.
`scripts/analysis/range_session_report.py` shows them per club against TT
("Carry-Varianten").

## Roll-out

Where the ball comes to rest is simulated too, starting from the flight's
touchdown: its velocity vector and its spin about the horizontal axis square to
the touchdown heading (positive = backspin; side spin does not enter). The model
is **Penner (2002)**, "The run of a golf ball", *Can. J. Phys.* 80: 931–940,
doi:10.1139/P02-035, with the paper's own values for one ground, a normal
fairway. Implemented in `src/openflight/rollout.py`.

Every impact follows Penner's eqs. (2)–(8). The ball digs a crater whose front
wall tilts the contact plane by the crater angle of eq. (8), with $v_i$ the
impact speed and $\phi$ the impact angle from the vertical:

$$
\theta_c = 15.4^\circ \cdot \frac{v_i}{18.6\ \text{m/s}} \cdot \frac{\phi}{44.4^\circ}
$$

The ball rebounds off that plane with the turf's restitution, which falls with
the normal impact speed $v_n$:

$$
e = 0.510 - 0.0375\,v_n + 0.000903\,v_n^2 \quad (v_n \le 20\ \text{m/s}),
\qquad e = 0.120 \quad \text{above}
$$

Friction either acts through the whole impact, so the ball slides out of it
($\mu$ below the critical $\mu_c$), or brings the contact point to rest first,
so the ball rolls out of it. Between impacts the ball flies under gravity alone
with its spin unchanged. Once a bounce would stay below 5 mm, the ball rolls to
rest at the constant deceleration of eq. (9):

$$
a = -\tfrac{5}{7}\,\rho\,g
$$

All of it happens in the vertical plane of the touchdown heading.

| Parameter | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kinetic friction ball–turf $\mu$ | 0.40 | Penner (2002), after Daish |
| Rolling friction $\rho$ | 0.131 | Penner (2002) |
| Critical bounce height | 5 mm | Penner (2002) |
| Restitution $e(v_n)$ | 0.510 − 0.0375 $v_n$ + 0.000903 $v_n^2$ up to 20 m/s, then 0.120 | Penner (2002) |
| Crater angle | 15.4° at 18.6 m/s and 44.4° from the vertical, scaled linearly | Haake's reference crater, Penner's eq. (8) |
| Impact speed limit | 40 m/s | OpenFlight: past it the linear crater fit turns low liners around, see below |

Penner used ρ = 0.131 throughout and matched empirical drive runs with it (his
Fig. 5). None of these values is fitted, offset or tuned here. The trends
follow the paper: more backspin gives less roll in general, a steeper landing
rolls less, and landing speed matters less and not monotonically, because the
crater deepens with speed.

**A ball that runs back.** A wedge with enough backspin bounces back towards the
tee. Penner computes its next impact with the spin still counted as backspin
against its new direction (Sect. 3.2, p. 938, the 9-iron of his Fig. 7): the
spin brakes the ball, which leaves the impact with top spin. The model keeps
that convention for a ball running back with backspin. A ball running back with
topspin, which the paper does not cover, is mirrored physically: motion and
spin both reverse. The side effect is a jump in the impact where the ball's
speed along the heading passes zero, so for wedges more spin can move the rest
point up to about half a metre further forward instead of back.

**The 40 m/s limit.** Eq. (8) scales one reference crater (Haake's, 15.4° at
18.6 m/s and 44.4°) linearly, so the crater wall always takes
$v_i / 53.6\ \text{m/s}$ of the impact angle: about three quarters at 40 m/s,
all of it at 53.6 m/s. Past 40 m/s the wall turns low liners around and they
run back. OpenFlight therefore gives a touchdown faster than 40 m/s
(`MAX_IMPACT_SPEED_MPS`) no roll, and its Total shows "—". In a sweep of 3,360
launches through the flight model (60–190 mph, 1–40°, 500–12,000 rpm) that is
171 shots, about 5 %: 159 thin shots and low liners launched at 1–10°, and 12
fast, high launches with almost no spin (160–190 mph, 30–40°, 500–1,500 rpm)
that come down steep and fast. The server logs such a shot as
`[SERVER] No roll: …` at INFO; any other failure of the model is a warning,
`[SERVER] Roll-out failed: …`.

**Where the bounds hold.** The tests pin Penner's cases below and keep spin-back
within a few metres: a steep 20 m/s landing, 20° from the vertical, with
1,500 rad/s rolls back 3.54 m against a tested bound of 5 m. That bound holds
for the touchdowns the flight model produces: in the sweep no shot that still
rolls runs back more than 1.44 m. It does not hold for every touchdown below
40 m/s: a steep 39 m/s landing with 1,300 rad/s, which no flight produces,
rolls back 8 m.

**On the shot.** Shot finalization rolls out every ballistic shot right after
its carry, so live, simulated and demo shots all get it; the provisional OPS
emit does not. Three fields travel with the shot and into the session log's
`shot_detected` entry:

- `total_yards`: the distance from the tee to the rest point, measured like
  carry (horizontal, `hypot(x, y)`);
- `roll_yards`: `total_yards` − carry, negative when the ball spins back;
- `roll_path`: `{"unit": "yd", "points": [[t, x, y, z], ...]}` in the frame and
  rounding of `flight_path`, from the touchdown (its first point is
  `flight_path`'s last) through the bounce apexes and landings and the roll to
  the rest point (its last point). `t` continues from the flight and strictly
  rises, `z` ≥ 0, at most 25 points (`MAX_ROLL_POINTS`), under 1 KB.

All three are `null` when the shot has no `flight_path` (a table carry, a
flight that ends at launch or never lands), when the ball lands faster than
40 m/s and when the model fails. The live payload rounds `total_yards` and
`roll_yards` to whole yards, like carry. Simulator connectors and the CSV
export do not use them.

## Inputs and fallbacks

The simulator needs ball speed, launch angle, and spin. Ball speed always comes
from the OPS243. The other two may be missing:

| Input | When present | When missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch angle | IWR6843 measurement | Falls back to the legacy carry table estimator |
| Spin | OPS243, if confidence is high | Club-typical spin by `ClubType`: TrackMan PGA Tour averages, or with the profile's ball type "range" (else `--typical-spin-table range-ball`) this user's Toptracer range-ball spin |

Measured spin is only trusted above `SPIN_CONFIDENCE_HIGH` (0.7). Below that,
the club-typical value is used — see
[rolling buffer & spin detection](rolling-buffer.md) for why.

`--typical-spin-table` (default `tour`) picks that club-typical value:
`tour` is the TrackMan PGA Tour table above (`CLUB_TYPICAL_SPIN_RPM`, from
`CLUB_PHYSICS`), `range-ball` this user's Toptracer range-ball spin
(`RANGE_BALL_SPIN_RPM`, from the 2026-11-03 study in
`analysis/flugmodell-20261103/spin`). The range-ball values are the TT median
spin per club over the TT sessions of 2026-10-02 to 2026-11-02 where a club has
at least 8 shots over at least 2 sessions (driver, 3- and 7-wood, 5- to 8-iron,
PW, SW), and are interpolated by nominal loft from the TT/tour ratio of the
neighbouring measured clubs for the rest; unknown clubs keep the tour value
5000 rpm. They sit far below the Tour averages for irons and wedges (8-iron
4260 vs 7500, PW 2310 vs 9000 rpm): leave-one-session-out on 308 OF/TT pairs,
carry MAE 14.3 → 12.5 m and apex MAE 5.6 → 4.8 m. Toptracer spin is itself an
estimate from the ball flight, not a measurement, and it varies a lot from day
to day (8-iron session medians 2952–7400 rpm), so the table is opt-in and
`CLUB_PHYSICS` stays the TrackMan default. The table is logged in the
session-start config (`typical_spin_table`, null with `--no-ballistics`).

`--typical-spin-table` is the device default. Each profile can override it in
the app (menu → Calibrate → **Ball type**): `standard` flies the `tour` table,
`range` the `range-ball` table (`profiles.BALL_TYPE_SPIN_TABLES`, the
`ball_type` profile setting, socket event `set_profile_ball_type`). A profile
that never chose keeps the launcher's table, so nothing changes until one
chooses, and the roster (`profiles` event) shows every profile's effective
`ball_type`. A shot flies the table of its attributed profile (else the active
one, like the driver tee height), on the live carry and the simulator's
fixed-density carry alike; a switch takes effect for the next shot, and a shot
flown again later (a late spin axis) keeps the table of its first flight. The
table a shot used is its `shot_detected` `typical_spin_table`, null when a
trusted measured spin was flown instead (`carry_spin_source`
`measured`/`calculated`); the session-start config keeps the launcher default.
Trusted measured spin still wins over either table.

`--typical-spin-scale` (default 1.0, allowed 0.5–1.2) multiplies that
club-typical value of either table for irons and wedges (`IRON_*`, PW/GW/SW/LW,
which the bag's `wedge-*` clubs map to) wherever it feeds the ballistic carry; the scaled number
is what `carry_spin_rpm` records and the UI shows as typical spin, and the
active factor is logged in the session-start config (`typical_spin_scale`, with
`typical_spin_scale_clubs: "irons_wedges"`). Driver, woods, hybrids and unknown
clubs keep the table value unchanged. Trusted measured spin is never scaled. The simulator connectors (GSPro etc.) keep their own unscaled per-club
spin model for the exported spin, and the legacy table fallback (no launch angle
or `--no-ballistics`) keeps its speed-based spin.

`--calculated-spin` forces the kinematic estimate
$170 \cdot v \cdot \sin(\text{LA})^{1.2}$ even when a measured value exists,
keeping the measured number in `spin_rpm_measured` for offline scoring.

Shot finalization in the server is the only place that writes
`carry_spin_adjusted` for a live shot: the simulator when it can run, the
spin table otherwise. The same committed number is what the kiosk shows and
what the simulator connectors receive. A simulated shot also stores its landing
offset from the target line as `carry_lateral_yards` (positive = right, seen
from behind the ball); the table estimator and shots without a horizontal
launch angle leave it empty.

The same simulation run provides the shot's `flight_path`, so a drawn flight
ends on the committed carry: `{"unit": "yd", "points": [[t, x, y, z], ...]}`,
with `t` in seconds since launch and `x` (downrange), `y` (lateral, positive =
right) and `z` (height) in yards. The points are the 0.05 s samples plus the
touchdown (`z` = 0) as the last one, every value rounded to 0.01 (about 3 KB
for a long drive). It is `null` for a table carry, for a flight that ends at
launch (a ball hit into the ground), for one still airborne at
`MAX_FLIGHT_SECONDS` and for one with a value that is not finite; swing-speed
reps have no such field. The path travels
with the shot and is written to the session log's `shot_detected` entry. The
history detail endpoint returns it, and the shot's `roll_path` with it, only on
request, as `/api/session-history/<id>?flight_path=1` (the paths are the bulk of
a shot's size, so the default payload stays small), and each only if it is
valid (unit `yd`, points of four finite numbers: 2–400 for the flight, 2–25 for
the roll), else `null`. `roll_yards` and `total_yards` come with every saved
shot, `null` where it has none.

## Disabling it

```bash
scripts/start-kiosk.sh --no-ballistics
```

Uses the legacy carry-table estimator for every shot. The simulator is the
default; shots without a vertical launch angle fall back to the table anyway.
